Left indicator — 4 alternatives

All share the same layout: full-row tap, indicator left of label, amber-50 wash on done. Only the indicator form changes.

Alt 1 — Notebook square

Rounded-sm square. Off: outlined. On: filled amber. Oldest marking convention.

Habits

Reads immediately as "markable slot." The amber fill has no ambiguity — slot + filled = done. Most likely to be understood without explanation. Less poetic than the others.

Alt 2 — Blossom icon

Reuses BlossomIcon. Creates continuity between habit-done and joy marking.

Habits

The same symbol already marks joy below this section. Using it here for habit-done makes the habit list feel like part of the same reflective gesture — marking, not ticking. Most Clarity-native of the four alternatives.

Alt 3 — Tally slash

Dash → diagonal stroke. Analog tally marking. Deliberately imperfect.

Habits

The horizontal dash in the off state reads as "pending" — a placeholder waiting to be crossed. The diagonal slash in the done state carries the physicality of a hand moving across paper. Most handmade-feeling of the four. The most likely to polarise: users who love analog tools will recognise it immediately; others may find it ambiguous.

Alt 4 — Minimal dot

8px dot, no border in either state. Off: stone-200. On: amber-500.

Habits

The dot is peripheral in the off state — barely registered, present but undemanding. When amber, it pulls attention without commanding it. The contrast between its small size and warm color is what makes it readable. This is the quietest of the four alternatives and pairs naturally with Proposal 4 (amber label) — the dot and the label together create a gentle double signal without either one overwhelming.

Which one?

Alt 2 (blossom) is the strongest design choice for Clarity specifically — it reuses an existing symbol with established meaning in the product and deepens the vocabulary around marking and feeling. The implementation is slightly heavier (SVG component) but justified.

Alt 4 (dot) is the strongest choice if you want the indicator to be peripheral — present but not competing with the label. Works best when combined with another signal (amber label text, row wash).

Alt 3 (slash) is the bravest. Worth trying if the team is committed to the analog sensibility and willing to accept a short learning curve for first-time users.

Alt 1 (square) is the safest. If there is any concern about discoverability or first-use confusion, start here — it will never be misread.
